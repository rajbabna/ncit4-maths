Part A — Perimeter & Area of Plane Shapes

1 Perimeter & Area — Rectangle and Triangle

Perimeter is the total distance around a shape (a length). Area is the amount of surface it covers (measured in square units, e.g. cm², m²).

Rectangle

Perimeter = 2(l + w)   ·   Area = l × w

Triangle

Area = ½ × base × perpendicular height   ·   Perimeter = a + b + c
Worked example. A rectangle is 12 cm long and 7 cm wide. Find its perimeter and area.

Perimeter = 2(12 + 7) = 2 × 19 = 38 cm
Area = 12 × 7 = 84 cm²

2 Circumference & Area of a Circle

The circumference is the distance around a circle. The radius r is half the diameter d.

Circumference C = 2πr = πd   ·   Area A = πr²

Use π ≈ 3.142 (or the π button on your calculator) unless told otherwise.

Worked example. A circle has radius 5 cm. Find (i) its circumference and (ii) its area (2 d.p.).

C = 2π(5) = 31.42 cm
A = π(5²) = 78.54 cm²

3 Area of a Parallelogram & Trapezium

Parallelogram

Area = base × perpendicular height

Trapezium

Area = ½(a + b) × h   where a and b are the parallel sides
Worked example. A trapezium has parallel sides 12 cm and 8 cm and height 5 cm. Find its area.

A = ½(12 + 8) × 5 = ½ × 20 × 5 = 50 cm²

Part B — Surface Area & Volume of Solids

4 Surface Area & Volume of Solids

Volume measures the space inside a solid (cubic units). Surface area is the total area of all its faces (square units).

Formulae given in the exam for the sphere, pyramid and cone are shown in the cards below; the cuboid, cylinder and prism formulae you should know.
Cuboid
SA = 2(lw + lh + wh)
V = l × w × h
Cylinder
SA = 2πr² + 2πrh
V = πr²h
Prism
SA = 2A + P × L
V = A × L

A = cross-section area, P = perimeter of cross-section, L = length

Sphere
Formula given SA = 4πr²
V = 4⁄3 πr³
Pyramid (square base)
Formula given SA = b² + 2bs
V = 1⁄3 b²h

b = base side, s = slant height, h = perpendicular height

Cone
Formula given SA = πr² + πrl
V = 1⁄3 πr²h

r = radius, l = slant height, h = perpendicular height

Slant height vs perpendicular height. For pyramids and cones the slant height (s or l) slants up the face and is used for surface area; the perpendicular height (h) goes straight up and is used for volume. Find one from the other with Pythagoras when needed.

Part C — Arcs & Sectors

5 Arc Length & Sector Area

An arc is part of a circle’s circumference. A sector (“pie slice”) is part of the circle’s area. Both are simple fractions of the whole circle.

Arc length = θ⁄360 × 2πr   ·   Sector area = θ⁄360 × πr²

where θ is the angle at the centre, in degrees. Also perimeter of a sector = arc length + 2r.

Worked example. A sector has radius 9 cm and angle 80°. Find the arc length and sector area (2 d.p.).

Arc = 80⁄360 × 2π(9) = 80⁄360 × 56.549 ≈ 12.57 cm
Sector area = 80⁄360 × π(9²) = 80⁄360 × 254.469 ≈ 56.55 cm²

Show full worked solutions

Q1. r = 3 m; A = πr² = 3.142 × 9 = 28.28 m².

Q2. V = A × L = 15 × 12 = 180 cm³.

Q3. Curved surface = πrl = 3.142 × 5 × 13 ≈ 204.2 cm².

Q4. Sector area = 120⁄360 × π(15²) = 235.7 cm².

Q5. V_cyl = πr²h = πr³ (since h = r); V_sphere = 4⁄3 πr³. Ratio = 1 ÷ 4⁄3 = ¾.
