Part A — Angles with Straight Lines

1 Angles on a Line, at a Point & Vertically Opposite

i · Angles on a straight line

Angles on a straight line add up to 180°. A straight line is a half-turn, and a half-turn is 180°.

ii · Angles at a point

Angles at a point add up to 360°. A full turn about a point is 360°.

iii · Vertically opposite angles

When two straight lines cross, the angles opposite each other are equal. Each is the “leftover” from 180° after subtracting the same angle, so they match.

Part B — Parallel Lines & Polygons

2 Angles Formed by Parallel Lines

When a transversal (a straight line) cuts two parallel lines, three useful pairs of angles appear.

Corresponding angles (F-shape)

Corresponding angles are equal. They sit in matching positions on the same side of the transversal.

Alternate angles (Z-shape)

Alternate angles are equal. They lie between the parallel lines on opposite sides of the transversal.

Co-interior angles (C/U-shape)

Co-interior (allied) angles add up to 180°. They lie between the parallel lines on the same side of the transversal.

3 Angle Properties of Triangles & Quadrilaterals

v · Triangle

The interior angles of a triangle add up to 180°. Any triangle is half of a parallelogram (or two right angles of a straight line).

Special triangles:

  • Equilateral — all three angles are 60°.
  • Isosceles — the two base angles are equal; the sides opposite them are equal.
  • Right-angled — one angle is 90°; the other two add to 90°.

An exterior angle of a triangle equals the sum of the two interior opposite angles.

v · Quadrilateral

The interior angles of a quadrilateral add up to 360°. A quadrilateral splits into two triangles, and 2 × 180° = 360°.

4 Angle Properties of Polygons

A polygon is a closed shape with straight sides. A polygon is regular when all its sides and all its angles are equal.

Key formulae

Sum of interior angles = (n − 2) × 180°, for n sides.
Sum of exterior angles = 360° (for any polygon).
For a regular polygon: each interior = (n − 2) × 180° ÷ n, and each exterior = 360° ÷ n.

Why (n − 2)? From one vertex you can draw diagonals that split the polygon into (n − 2) triangles, and each triangle has 180°.

Interior angle + exterior angle = 180° at every vertex.

Part C — Circle Theorems

5 Circle Theorems: Semi-circle & Tangent–Radius

vii · Angle in a semi-circle

The angle in a semi-circle is 90° (a right angle). It is a special case of the centre theorem: the diameter subtends 180° at the centre, so the circumference angle is 180° ÷ 2 = 90°.

viii · Angle between a tangent and a radius

A tangent meets a radius at 90° at the point of contact. The radius to the point of contact is perpendicular to the tangent.

6 Centre, Circumference & Segments

ix · Angle at the centre

The angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference, when both stand on the same arc. This is the “master” circle theorem — the semi-circle result is its special case.

x · Angles in the same segment

Angles in the same segment (subtended by the same chord from the same side) are equal. Each is half of the same central angle.

xi · Angles in opposite segments

Angles in opposite segments are supplementary — the opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add up to 180°. The four vertices lie on the circle (a cyclic quadrilateral).
