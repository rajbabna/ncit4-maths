A short lesson

An equation has one answer.
An inequality has a neighborhood.

Solving 2x + 3 < 11 doesn't land on a single number — it lands on every number that makes the statement true. This lesson shows you how to find that neighborhood, and the one rule that trips almost everyone up at least once.

01

The four symbols

Every linear inequality uses one of these. Notice the two on the right include "or equal to" — that changes whether the boundary point counts.

<
Less than
Boundary not included. Open circle on a number line.
>
Greater than
Boundary not included. Open circle on a number line.
≤
Less than or equal to
Boundary included. Closed (filled) circle.
≥
Greater than or equal to
Boundary included. Closed (filled) circle.
02

Adding and subtracting work exactly like equations

For addition and subtraction, an inequality behaves precisely like an equation. Do the same thing to both sides, and the inequality symbol never changes.

Equation
x + 5 = 12
x + 5 − 5 = 12 − 5 subtract 5
x = 7
Inequality
x + 5 > 12
x + 5 − 5 > 12 − 5 subtract 5
x > 7
