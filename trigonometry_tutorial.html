Part A — Right-angled Triangles

1 Pythagoras’ Theorem

In a right-angled triangle, the side opposite the right angle is the hypotenuse (the longest side). Pythagoras’ theorem relates the three sides:

a² + b² = c²   where c is the hypotenuse
How to use it.
  • To find the hypotenuse: c = √(a² + b²) — add.
  • To find a shorter side: a = √(c² − b²) — subtract.

2 SOHCAHTOA — Sides & Angles

For an acute angle θ in a right-angled triangle we label the sides relative to θ:

  • Hypotenuse (H) — opposite the right angle (always the longest).
  • Opposite (O) — opposite the angle θ.
  • Adjacent (A) — next to θ (and not the hypotenuse).
SOH sin θ = O / H  ·  CAH cos θ = A / H  ·  TOA tan θ = O / A

The ratios sin θ, cos θ and tan θ depend only on the size of the angle — not on how big the triangle is.

i · Finding a side

Choose the ratio that links the side you know to the side you want. e.g. given H and θ, use sin θ = O/H to find O; given A and θ, use cos θ = A/H to find H.

i · Finding an angle

Use the inverse trig function, e.g. θ = sin⁻¹(O/H). Make sure your calculator is in DEGREE mode. Give angles to 1 decimal place.
Worked example. A ladder leans against a wall. Its foot is 2.5 m from the wall and it makes an angle of 68° with the ground. How long is the ladder?

cos 68° = 2.5 / L → L = 2.5 / cos 68° ≈ 6.7 m (1 d.p.)

Part B — Trigonometric Problems in 2-D

3 Angles of Elevation & Depression

Angle of elevation

The angle measured upward from the horizontal to an object above you.

Angle of depression

The angle measured downward from the horizontal to an object below you.
Key fact: The angle of elevation from A to B equals the angle of depression from B to A — they are alternate angles between two horizontal lines.

4 Trigonometry with Bearings

Bearings questions combine directions with distances, so they are solved with Pythagoras or the trig ratios (for right-angled triangles), or the sine and cosine rules (for other triangles).

Method
  1. Sketch a North line at each point involved.
  2. Mark the given bearings and distances.
  3. Find the angle inside the triangle by subtracting/adding the bearings at the same point.
  4. Choose Pythagoras, SOHCAHTOA, or the sine/cosine rules and solve.
  5. Convert any final direction back into a three-figure bearing.
Worked example. A boat sails 5 km from O on a bearing of 000° (due North) to P, then 12 km on a bearing of 090° (due East) to Q. Find the bearing of Q from O.

The angle at O is 90°, so OQ = √(5² + 12²) = 13 km.
tan θ = 12 / 5 → θ ≈ 67.4° east of north → bearing of Q from O = 067.4°.

Part C — Sine & Cosine Rules and Area

5 The Sine Rule

The sine rule works in any triangle (not just right-angled ones). Sides a, b, c are opposite angles A, B, C respectively.

a / sin A = b / sin B = c / sin C   or   sin A / a = sin B / b = sin C / c

Use it when you know a matching side-and-angle pair plus one more side or angle.

Obtuse angles and sin. For 0° < θ < 180°, sin θ = sin(180° − θ). So a sine-rule angle may have two possible values (an acute and an obtuse one). Check whether an obtuse angle is possible in the triangle.

6 The Cosine Rule

The cosine rule works in any triangle. Use it when you know two sides and the included angle (to find the third side), or all three sides (to find an angle).

a² = b² + c² − 2bc cos A   ·   cos A = (b² + c² − a²) / 2bc

7 Area of a Triangle = ½ ab sin C

When you know two sides and the angle between them, use:

Area = ½ × a × b × sin C

where C is the included angle between sides a and b.

Check it makes sense. With C = 90°, sin C = 1 and the formula becomes ½ab — exactly the right-angled triangle formula. With the angle close to 0° or 180°, sin C ≈ 0 so the area is nearly zero (a very flat triangle).
