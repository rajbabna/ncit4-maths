D1 Single Events — 10 Marks

The Core Formula

Probability measures how likely an event is to happen. It always falls between 0 (impossible) and 1 (certain).

P(event) = Number of favourable outcomes ÷ Total possible outcomes
Example — D1.1: Fair Die (6 sides)

a) P(odd number) = 3 ÷ 6 = 1/2  → favourable outcomes: {1, 3, 5}
b) P(number > 2) = 4 ÷ 6 = 2/3  → favourable outcomes: {3, 4, 5, 6}
c) P(number 7) = 0 ÷ 6 = 0  → impossible, a die only goes up to 6

A probability can never be greater than 1 or less than 0. If you get an answer outside this range, check your working!

Coloured Balls — D1.2

Bag contains 5 red + 3 blue + 2 green = 10 balls total. One drawn at random.

P(red) = 5/10 = 1/2   |   P(not blue) = 7/10
Two ways to find P(not blue)

Method 1 (direct): balls that are not blue = 5 red + 2 green = 7. So P(not blue) = 7/10.
Method 2 (complement): P(not blue) = 1 − P(blue) = 1 − 3/10 = 7/10. Both give the same answer!

  • Count all items in the sample space (always do this first)
  • Identify the favourable outcomes for your event
  • Write the fraction and simplify if possible

The Complement Rule — D1.3

The complement of event E is "E does not happen". Together they cover all possibilities, so their probabilities add to exactly 1.

P(E does not occur) = 1 − P(E)
Example — D1.3

If P(E) = 0.35, then P(E does not occur) = 1 − 0.35 = 0.65

💡 Think: "everything else"
Useful when NOT is easier to count
Always check: P(E) + P(E') = 1
D2 Combined Events — 15 Marks

Possibility Diagram — Two Coins (D2.1)

When two or more events happen together, list all combinations systematically. For two coins, each can land H or T:

Coin 2 → HCoin 2 → T
Coin 1 ↓ HHH ✓HT
Coin 1 ↓ TTHTT
Total outcomes = 4  |  P(two heads) = 1/4  |  P(at least one tail) = 3/4
Key insight — "at least one tail"

"At least one tail" includes HT, TH, TT = 3 outcomes. Or use the complement: 1 − P(no tails) = 1 − 1/4 = 3/4. The complement method is often faster!

"Both same" — the diagonal

"Both same" means (1,1), (2,2), (3,3), (4,4) — that's the main diagonal of the grid. Count them: 4 outcomes out of 16.

Tree Diagram — Without Replacement (D2.3)

Bag: 4 red, 3 blue (7 total). Draw two balls without replacing the first. Probabilities change after the first draw!

P(two red) = 4/7 × 3/6 = 12/42 = 2/7
P(one of each) = P(R,B) + P(B,R) = 2/7 + 2/7 = 4/7
Why probabilities change on second draw

After drawing 1 red ball, only 3 red remain in 6 total balls. Always update both the colour count AND the total when the problem says "without replacement".

  • Draw the first branches with original bag probabilities
  • For each second branch: reduce total by 1, and reduce the drawn colour by 1
  • Multiply along branches (AND = ×) to get end-branch probabilities
  • Add matching end-branches for combined outcomes (OR = +)
D3 Problem Solving — 10 Marks

Multiple Choice Guessing — D3.1

4 questions, each with 4 options. Guessing randomly. P(correct on one question) = 1/4. P(wrong on one) = 3/4.

To find P(at least 1 correct) — use the complement. It is much easier than listing every possibility.

P(at least 1 correct) = 1 − P(none correct)
Step-by-step solution

Step 1: P(all 4 wrong) = 3/4 × 3/4 × 3/4 × 3/4 = (3/4)⁴ = 81/256
Step 2: P(at least 1 correct) = 1 − 81/256 = 175/256 ≈ 0.684

"At least 1" problems almost always use the complement strategy. Calculating P(exactly 1) + P(exactly 2) + P(exactly 3) + P(exactly 4) is much harder and more error-prone.

Venn Diagrams & Set Operations — D3.2

Class of 30. Football (F) = 12, Netball (N) = 15, Both = 8. Find P(play either but not both).

Football only = 12 − 8 = 4
Netball only = 15 − 8 = 7
Either but not both = 4 + 7 = 11   → P = 11/30
  • Draw the Venn diagram with two overlapping circles inside a rectangle (the universal set)
  • Place the "both" count in the intersection (overlap region): 8
  • Subtract: Football only = 12 − 8 = 4; Netball only = 15 − 8 = 7
  • Neither = 30 − (4 + 8 + 7) = 11 (plays neither sport)
  • P(either but not both) = (4 + 7) ÷ 30 = 11/30
