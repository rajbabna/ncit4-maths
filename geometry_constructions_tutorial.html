1 Measuring Lines and Angles

Measuring Lines

A line segment is a part of a line with two definite endpoints. Its length is measured using a ruler (also called a straightedge with markings).

Key Rule: Always place the zero mark of the ruler exactly at one endpoint, keep the ruler flat, and read off the measurement at the other endpoint. Measurements are usually given in millimetres (mm) or centimetres (cm).

Measuring Angles

An angle is formed when two rays share a common endpoint called the vertex. Angles are measured in degrees (°) using a protractor.

How to measure an angle with a protractor
  1. Place the centre hole of the protractor exactly on the vertex of the angle.
  2. Align the baseline (0°) of the protractor with one arm of the angle.
  3. Read the scale where the second arm of the angle crosses the protractor.
  4. Choose the correct scale (inner or outer) — start from 0° on the arm you aligned.
Angle Types:
Acute: 0° – 90°  |  Right: 90°  |  Obtuse: 90° – 180°  |  Straight: 180°  |  Reflex: 180° – 360°

2 Constructing Simple Geometrical Figures

Simple geometrical figures — such as triangles, rectangles, and parallelograms — can be constructed from given data (lengths of sides and sizes of angles) using a ruler, protractor, and set squares.

Constructing a Triangle (Given Side + Two Angles)

Example: Construct △ABC with AB = 7 cm, ∠A = 60°, ∠B = 50°
  1. Draw base line AB = 7 cm using a ruler.
  2. At point A, use a protractor to mark an angle of 60° and draw a ray.
  3. At point B, use a protractor to mark an angle of 50° and draw a ray in the opposite direction.
  4. The intersection of the two rays is point C. Label it.
  5. The third angle ∠C = 180° − 60° − 50° = 70°.

Using a Set Square

A set square is a triangular drawing tool. It is used to:

  • Draw perpendicular lines (lines at 90°) quickly.
  • Draw parallel lines when slid along a ruler.
  • The 45° set square has angles 45° – 45° – 90°.
  • The 30–60° set square has angles 30° – 60° – 90°.

3 Angle Bisectors & Perpendicular Bisectors

Angle Bisector

An angle bisector is a ray that divides an angle into two equal halves.

Constructing an angle bisector with a protractor
  1. Measure the full angle using a protractor. Say the angle is 80°.
  2. Divide by 2: half-angle = 40°.
  3. From the vertex, draw a ray at 40° from one arm. This is the bisector.

Perpendicular Bisector

The perpendicular bisector of a line segment is a line that:

  • Crosses the segment at its midpoint, and
  • Is perpendicular to it (makes a 90° angle).
Constructing a perpendicular bisector with ruler & protractor
  1. Draw segment AB. Measure its length and find the midpoint M.
  2. Mark point M on the segment.
  3. Place the protractor at M, aligned with AB, and mark 90° on both sides.
  4. Draw a line through M at 90° to AB. This is the perpendicular bisector.

4 Scale Drawings

A scale drawing is an accurate drawing of a real object where all measurements are reduced (or enlarged) by the same factor called the scale.

Scale Format: A scale is written as a ratio, e.g. 1 : 100, which means 1 unit on the drawing represents 100 units in real life. So 1 cm on the drawing = 100 cm (1 m) in real life.

Reading a Scale Drawing

To find the real measurement from a drawing:

Formula
  1. Measure the length on the drawing (in cm).
  2. Multiply by the scale factor: Real length = Drawing length × Scale
  3. Convert units if needed.

Example: A room on a scale drawing measures 4.5 cm. The scale is 1 : 200.
Real length = 4.5 × 200 = 900 cm = 9 m.

Making a Scale Drawing

Steps
  1. Choose an appropriate scale (e.g., 1 : 50).
  2. Divide all real measurements by the scale factor to get drawing measurements.
  3. Draw accurately using a ruler and protractor, labelling the scale used.

5 Constructing a Triangle Given Three Sides

Examiner's Rule: When constructing a triangle given three sides, you must use a ruler and compasses only — no protractors or set squares for this construction.

Suppose you need to construct △ABC with sides AB = 7 cm, BC = 5 cm, CA = 6 cm.

Step-by-step construction (ruler & compasses only)
  1. Draw the base: Draw AB = 7 cm using a ruler.
  2. Arc from A: Open the compasses to 6 cm (= CA). Place the compass point at A and draw an arc above the line.
  3. Arc from B: Open the compasses to 5 cm (= BC). Place the compass point at B and draw an arc to intersect the first arc.
  4. Mark C: The intersection point of the two arcs is vertex C.
  5. Complete the triangle: Join A to C and B to C with a ruler.
Remember: Always leave your construction arcs visible in an exam — erasing them costs marks! They are your evidence of method.

Triangle Inequality

For any triangle, the sum of any two sides must be greater than the third side. If this condition is not met, the arcs will not intersect and no triangle can be formed.

Check: 6 + 5 = 11 > 7 ✔  |  7 + 5 = 12 > 6 ✔  |  7 + 6 = 13 > 5 ✔
