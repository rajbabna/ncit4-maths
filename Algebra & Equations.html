Laws of Indices

Rules for working with positive, negative, zero, and fractional powers

Key Laws

LawRuleExample
Multiplicationaᵐ × aⁿ = aᵐ⁺ⁿ3² × 3⁴ = 3⁶
Divisionaᵐ ÷ aⁿ = aᵐ⁻ⁿ5⁷ ÷ 5³ = 5⁴
Power of a power(aᵐ)ⁿ = aᵐⁿ(2³)⁴ = 2¹²
Zero indexa⁰ = 17⁰ = 1
Negative indexa⁻ⁿ = 1/aⁿ4⁻² = 1/16
Fractional indexa^(1/n) = ⁿ√a8^(1/3) = 2
Fractional index (general)a^(m/n) = (ⁿ√a)ᵐ8^(2/3) = 4
💡
Remember: The base must be the same before you can apply the multiplication or division law.
Example 1Simplify 2⁵ × 2⁻²
Simplify: 2⁵ × 2⁻²
1
Same base (2), so add the indices.
2⁵ × 2⁻² = 2^(5 + (−2))
2
= 2³
3
Evaluate: 2³ = 8
✓Answer: 2³ = 8
Example 2Evaluate 27^(2/3)
Evaluate: 27^(2/3)
1
Rewrite using the fractional index rule: a^(m/n) = (ⁿ√a)ᵐ
2
27^(2/3) = (∛27)²
3
Find the cube root: ∛27 = 3
4
Square the result: 3² = 9
✓Answer: 9
Example 3Simplify (x³y²) ÷ (x y⁵)
Simplify: x³y² ÷ xy⁵
1
Divide powers of x: x³ ÷ x¹ = x^(3−1) = x²
2
Divide powers of y: y² ÷ y⁵ = y^(2−5) = y⁻³
3
Write with a positive index: y⁻³ = 1/y³
✓Answer: x²/y³

Algebraic Expressions

Addition, subtraction, multiplication and division of simple expressions

Core Principles

Like terms share the exact same variable(s) and power(s). Only like terms can be added or subtracted.

Expanding brackets: multiply every term inside by the term outside.

FOIL (for two binomials): First · Outer · Inner · Last.

Example 1Add & Subtract
Simplify: 4x² + 3x − x² − 7x + 5
1
Group like terms: (4x² − x²) + (3x − 7x) + 5
2
3x² − 4x + 5
✓Answer: 3x² − 4x + 5
Example 2Expand and simplify
Expand and simplify: 3(2x − 4) − 2(x + 1)
1
Expand both brackets: 6x − 12 − 2x − 2
2
Collect like terms: (6x − 2x) + (−12 − 2)
3
4x − 14
✓Answer: 4x − 14
Example 3Multiply two binomials (FOIL)
Expand: (2x + 3)(x − 4)
1
First: 2x × x = 2x²
2
Outer: 2x × (−4) = −8x
3
Inner: 3 × x = 3x
4
Last: 3 × (−4) = −12
5
Combine: 2x² − 8x + 3x − 12 = 2x² − 5x − 12
✓Answer: 2x² − 5x − 12

Linear Equations

Solving equations of the form ax + b = c with one unknown

Strategy

Isolate the unknown by performing the same operation on both sides. Work in reverse order of operations: undo addition/subtraction first, then multiplication/division.

Example 1Basic linear equation
Solve: 3x + 7 = 22
1
Subtract 7 from both sides: 3x = 22 − 7 = 15
2
Divide by 3: x = 15 ÷ 3 = 5
3
Check: 3(5) + 7 = 15 + 7 = 22 ✓
✓x = 5
Example 2Variables on both sides
Solve: 5x − 3 = 2x + 9
1
Move x terms left: 5x − 2x = 9 + 3
2
Simplify: 3x = 12
3
Divide: x = 4
✓x = 4
Example 3Equation with brackets
Solve: 4(x − 2) = 2(x + 5)
1
Expand: 4x − 8 = 2x + 10
2
Move x terms: 4x − 2x = 10 + 8
3
2x = 18 → x = 9
✓x = 9

Fractional Equations

Equations with numerical and linear algebraic denominators

Strategy

Find the Lowest Common Denominator (LCD) of all fractions, then multiply every term on both sides by the LCD to clear the fractions. Solve the resulting linear equation.

⚠️
Check your answer! Always substitute back to make sure no denominator equals zero.
Example 1Numerical denominator
Solve: x/3 + x/4 = 7
1
LCD of 3 and 4 is 12. Multiply through by 12.
2
12 · (x/3) + 12 · (x/4) = 12 · 7
3
4x + 3x = 84
4
7x = 84 → x = 12
✓x = 12
Example 2Algebraic denominator
Solve: 3/(x + 1) = 1/(x − 2)
1
Cross-multiply: 3(x − 2) = 1(x + 1)
2
Expand: 3x − 6 = x + 1
3
Collect: 2x = 7 → x = 3.5
4
Check: denominators are 4.5 and 1.5 — neither is zero ✓
✓x = 3.5
5

Simultaneous Linear Equations

Two equations, two unknowns — elimination and substitution methods

Two Methods

Elimination: make the coefficient of one variable equal in both equations, then add or subtract to eliminate it.

Substitution: rearrange one equation to express x (or y) in terms of the other variable, then substitute into the second equation.

Example 1Elimination Method
Solve: 2x + 3y = 12 and 4x − 3y = 6
1
The y-coefficients are +3 and −3. Add the equations to eliminate y:
(2x + 4x) + (3y − 3y) = 12 + 6
2
6x = 18 → x = 3
3
Substitute x = 3 into equation 1:
2(3) + 3y = 12 → 3y = 6 → y = 2
✓x = 3, y = 2
Example 2Substitution Method
Solve: y = 2x − 1 and 3x + y = 14
1
y is already expressed in terms of x. Substitute into equation 2:
3x + (2x − 1) = 14
2
Simplify: 5x − 1 = 14 → 5x = 15 → x = 3
3
Find y: y = 2(3) − 1 = 5
✓x = 3, y = 5
6

Quadratic Equations

Factorisation · Quadratic Formula · Completing the Square

Three Methods

Factorisation: write ax² + bx + c as a product of two brackets and apply the zero-product property.

Quadratic formula: x = (−b ± √(b²−4ac)) / 2a

Completing the square: rewrite ax² + bx + c = 0 as (x + p)² = q, then take the square root.

Example 1By factorisation
Solve: x² − 7x + 12 = 0
1
Find two numbers that multiply to 12 and add to −7: −3 and −4
2
Factorise: (x − 3)(x − 4) = 0
3
Zero-product property: x − 3 = 0 → x = 3 or x − 4 = 0 → x = 4
✓x = 3 or x = 4
Example 2By the quadratic formula
Solve: 2x² − 5x − 3 = 0
1
Identify: a = 2, b = −5, c = −3
2
Discriminant: b² − 4ac = 25 + 24 = 49
3
x = (5 ± √49) / 4 = (5 ± 7) / 4
4
x = 12/4 = 3 or x = −2/4 = −1/2
✓x = 3 or x = −½
Example 3By completing the square
Solve: x² + 6x − 7 = 0
1
Move constant: x² + 6x = 7
2
Add (6/2)² = 9 to both sides: x² + 6x + 9 = 16
3
Left side is a perfect square: (x + 3)² = 16
4
Take square root: x + 3 = ±4
5
x = 1 or x = −7
✓x = 1 or x = −7

Linear Inequalities

Solving and representing inequalities on a number line

Key Rules

Solve like a linear equation — but with one important difference:

📌 Multiplying or dividing by a negative number reverses the inequality sign.

ℹ️
Notation: Use ● (filled dot) for ≤ or ≥ and ○ (open dot) for < or > on a number line.
Example 1Simple inequality
Solve: 4x − 3 ≥ 9
1
Add 3: 4x ≥ 12
2
Divide by 4 (positive — sign stays): x ≥ 3
✓x ≥ 3
Example 2Dividing by a negative number
Solve: −2x + 5 > 1
1
Subtract 5: −2x > −4
2
Divide by −2 (reverse the sign!): x < 2
✓x < 2
Example 3Combined (double) inequality
Solve: −1 ≤ 2x + 3 < 9
1
Subtract 3 throughout: −4 ≤ 2x < 6
2
Divide by 2: −2 ≤ x < 3
✓−2 ≤ x < 3
