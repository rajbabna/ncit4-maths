01 — Building Blocks

Foundations

Before diving into permutations and combinations, you need to understand three foundational ideas that underpin all counting problems.

Factorial ( n! )

The factorial of a non-negative integer n is the product of all positive integers up to and including n.

Definition
n! = n × (n−1) × (n−2) × … × 2 × 1     and     0! = 1
Calculate 5! and 3!
1
5! = 5 × 4 × 3 × 2 × 1 = 120
2
3! = 3 × 2 × 1 = 6
3
Note: 0! = 1 by definition — a special but important case.

Fundamental Counting Principle

If Event A can occur in m ways and Event B can occur in n ways, then both events together can occur in m × n ways.

A restaurant offers 4 starters and 6 mains. How many different 2-course meals are possible?
1
Starters: 4 choices
2
Mains: 6 choices
3
Total meals: 4 × 6 = 24
✓ Answer: 24 different meal combinations
02 — Order Matters

Permutations

A permutation is an arrangement of items where the order in which they are placed matters. Swapping two items creates a different permutation.

All n Objects

The number of ways to arrange ALL n distinct objects in a row:

Formula
P = n!
In how many ways can 4 students stand in a line for a photo?
1
We arrange all 4 students, so n = 4
2
P = 4! = 4 × 3 × 2 × 1 = 24
✓ Answer: 24 different arrangements

Choosing r from n — nPr

The number of ways to choose AND arrange r items from a group of n distinct items:

Formula
nPr = n! / (n − r)!
From 8 athletes, how many ways can Gold, Silver, and Bronze medals be awarded?
1
n = 8 athletes, r = 3 medals → order matters (Gold ≠ Silver)
2
8P3 = 8! / (8−3)! = 8! / 5!
3
= (8 × 7 × 6 × 5!) / 5! = 8 × 7 × 6 = 336
✓ Answer: 336 ways to award the medals

Permutations with Repetition

When items can be reused, the number of arrangements of r items from n is:

Formula
P = nr
How many 3-digit PIN codes are possible using digits 0–9?
1
n = 10 (digits 0–9), r = 3 positions, repetition allowed
2
P = 10³ = 1,000
✓ Answer: 1,000 possible PINs

Permutations with Identical Objects

When some items are identical, divide by the factorials of each repeated count:

Formula
P = n! / (p! × q! × r! × …)
How many distinct arrangements are there of the letters in MISSISSIPPI?
1
Total letters n = 11
2
Repeats: M×1, I×4, S×4, P×2
3
P = 11! / (1! × 4! × 4! × 2!)
4
= 39,916,800 / (1 × 24 × 24 × 2) = 34,650
✓ Answer: 34,650 distinct arrangements

Circular Permutations

When arranging n objects in a circle, one position is fixed to remove duplicate rotations:

Formula
Circular P = (n − 1)!
In how many ways can 6 people be seated around a round table?
1
n = 6 people, circular arrangement
2
P = (6−1)! = 5! = 120
✓ Answer: 120 circular arrangements
03 — Order Doesn't Matter

Combinations

A combination is a selection of items where only which items are chosen matters — not the order. {A, B} and {B, A} count as the same combination.

Choosing r from n — nCr

Also written as C(n, r) or ⁿCᵣ or "n choose r":

Formula
nCr = n! / (r! × (n − r)!)
A class of 10 students needs to choose a committee of 3. How many committees are possible?
1
n = 10, r = 3 → order doesn't matter (committee, not ranked positions)
2
10C3 = 10! / (3! × 7!)
3
= (10 × 9 × 8) / (3 × 2 × 1) = 720 / 6 = 120
✓ Answer: 120 possible committees
A hand of 5 cards is dealt from a standard 52-card deck. How many different hands are possible?
1
n = 52, r = 5 → order of cards in hand doesn't matter
2
52C5 = 52! / (5! × 47!)
3
= (52 × 51 × 50 × 49 × 48) / (5 × 4 × 3 × 2 × 1) = 2,598,960
✓ Answer: 2,598,960 possible hands

Combinations with Repetition

When you can choose the same item more than once:

Formula
C = (n + r − 1)! / (r! × (n − 1)!)   =   C(n + r − 1, r)
An ice cream shop has 5 flavours. How many ways can you choose 3 scoops (repetition allowed, order of scoops doesn't matter)?
1
n = 5, r = 3, repetition allowed, order doesn't matter
2
C = C(5 + 3 − 1, 3) = C(7, 3)
3
= 7! / (3! × 4!) = 210 / 6 = 35
✓ Answer: 35 different ways

Pascal's Triangle

Each number in Pascal's Triangle equals the sum of the two numbers directly above it. Row n, position r gives the value of nCr.

Row 0: 1
Row 1: 1   1
Row 2: 1   2   1
Row 3: 1   3   3   1
Row 4: 1   4   6   4   1
Row 5: 1   5   10   10   5   1
Verify that 4C2 = 6 using Pascal's Triangle.
1
Row 4, position 2 (0-indexed) in the triangle = 6
2
Check: 4C2 = 4!/(2!×2!) = 24/(2×2) = 6 ✓
05 — Real-World Uses

Applications

Does order matter?
YES → USE
Permutation nPr
NO → USE
Combination nCr
🔐 How many 4-letter passwords (no repeats) can be made from the 26 English letters?
1
Order matters (ABCD ≠ DCBA) → Permutation
2
26P4 = 26!/(26−4)! = 26 × 25 × 24 × 23 = 358,800
✓ Answer: 358,800 passwords
⚽ A football squad has 20 players. How many ways can a team of 11 be selected?
1
Order doesn't matter (just who plays) → Combination
2
20C11 = 20! / (11! × 9!) = 167,960
✓ Answer: 167,960 possible teams
🎲 What is the probability of picking 2 red balls from a bag of 5 red and 3 blue balls?
1
Favourable outcomes: Choose 2 red from 5 → 5C2 = 10
2
Total outcomes: Choose any 2 from 8 → 8C2 = 28
3
P = 10 / 28 = 5/14 ≈ 0.357
✓ Answer: 5/14 ≈ 35.7%
